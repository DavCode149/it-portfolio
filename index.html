IT Support Specialist

An IT Support Specialist helps individuals and organizations solve problems related to computers, software, and networks. In Ontario, these professionals are essential in schools, hospitals, government offices, and businesses.

Their main responsibility is to keep technology working properly so that users can perform their daily tasks without disruption. This role combines technical knowledge with strong communication skills.

A typical day includes responding to help desk tickets, diagnosing system problems, installing software, and assisting users both in person and remotely.

Daily Tasks
Fixing computers, updating software, helping users
Typical Day
Support requests, system maintenance, troubleshooting

Education & Training (Ontario)

Students in Ontario can begin preparing for this career in high school by choosing courses related to technology and mathematics. These subjects help build problem-solving and computer skills.

Recommended high school courses include Computer Science (ICS), Mathematics, Business Studies, and Communications Technology. These courses introduce students to coding, systems, and digital tools.

After high school, most IT Support Specialists attend college or university for Information Technology or Computer Systems programs. Certifications such as CompTIA A+ and Network+ improve job prospects.

High School
ICS, Math, Business, Tech
Post-Secondary
College diplomas or university degrees

Skills & Qualities Needed

IT Support Specialists need technical knowledge of computer hardware, operating systems, and basic networking. These skills help them identify and fix issues efficiently.

Strong communication skills are required to explain technical problems clearly to non-technical users. Patience and professionalism are essential when helping frustrated clients.

Technical Skills
Hardware, OS, networking
Personal Traits
Patience, teamwork, responsibility

Salary & Earnings (Ontario)

Entry-level IT Support Specialists in Ontario earn approximately $40,000 to $50,000 per year. With experience, salaries can increase to $60,000 to $80,000 or more.

Pay is usually salary-based, but some positions are hourly. Earnings vary by city, employer, and level of expertise.

Entry Level
$40k–$50k
Experienced
$60k–$80k+

Work Environment

IT Support Specialists work indoors in offices, schools, hospitals, and data centers. Some positions allow remote support through online systems.

Work hours are usually daytime, but shift work and on-call duties may be required in certain industries.

Workplace
Offices, schools, hospitals
Hours
Day shifts, some on-call

Extras

This career is important because modern society depends on technology. IT Support Specialists ensure that systems stay functional and secure.

Benefits include high demand and career growth opportunities, while challenges include handling stressful technical problems and user frustration.

Tools and technologies include computers, servers, operating systems, and networking devices. Support software is used to manage technical issues efficiently.

A fun fact is that the first recorded computer bug was an actual moth found inside a machine in 1947.
